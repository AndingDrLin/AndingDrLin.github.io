The Flyback Converter (VOUT <> VIN).




• The basic operation of the flyback converter is similar to that of the buck-boost
  converter.
• By placing a second winding on the inductor, it is possible to achieve electrical
  isolation. Hence, a transformer is placed with 𝑁1 turns with inductance (𝐿𝑝 ) on
  primary winding and 𝑁2 turns with inductance (𝐿𝑠 ) are placed on secondary winding
• When switch is closed, the current is increasing linearly in the physical primary
  winding, and the inductor core flux increases linearly from its initial value. However,
  due to the polarity of the secondary winding of transformer no current flows in
  secondary winding.
• As soon the switch is open, the voltage across inductor on primary side reverses,
  and due to opposite polarity of winding in secondary, diodes turns on and current
  flow due to stored energy in the core.

  Protection of Switching Devices and Circuits
• The function of a snubber circuit is to reduce the electrical stresses
  placed on a device during switching by a power electronics
  converter to levels that are within the electrical ratings of the
  device.
• It must be emphasized that snubbers are not a fundamental part
  of a power electronic converter circuit. The snubber circuit is an
  addition to the basic converter, which is added to reduce the
  stresses on an electrical component, usually a power
  semiconductor device.

There are two key points to note about snubber circuit design:
1. There is always a degree of experimentation needed to tune a snubber’s
   operation. It is impossible to precisely design the circuit (although the desired
   function can be predicted).
2. The final circuit will probably be a compromise. A capacitive snubber that
   controls a voltage spike at turn-off may cause a current spike at turn-on.
   Similarly, controlling a current spike on one edge may cause a voltage spike on
   the other.
